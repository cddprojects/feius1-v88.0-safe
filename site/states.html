States

The floor changes at the state line

A national average and a federal minimum describe different maps. The higher rate, where one exists, is the one that applies.

Weekline · Record · Reviewed 3 November 2026

In short

  • The federal minimum wage is still $7.25 an hour, unchanged since 24 July 2009.
  • The Department of Labor table dated 1 July 2026 lists 30 states and the District of Columbia above that floor.
  • A separate compilation, checked to 13 October 2026, found pay-range posting rules in 14 states and the District.
  • A contract label on a posting does not, by itself, decide legal status.

Two wage maps

Employers covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act pay the highest of the federal, state, and local rates. On the 1 July 2026 consolidated table, Washington was $17.13, California $16.90, New York City and Long Island $17.00, upstate New York $16.00, and Connecticut $16.94. Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Missouri, Nebraska, and Delaware were listed at $15.00. Thirteen states matched $7.25, including Texas and Pennsylvania. Seven had no state minimum or a lower one, so the federal $7.25 still applies. City rates can sit above the state and are not in that table.

Florida was listed at $14.00 on the July table. A state notice had said the rate would rise to $15 on 30 September 2026. The July table predates that step, so this page does not treat $15 as confirmed by a later federal table. The District of Columbia row on the same Labor Department page says $18.40, while a note on the page says $17.50. The District’s own office is the place to settle that figure.

What the averages say, and what an ad says

In September 2026, preliminary and seasonally adjusted, average hourly earnings for all private employees were $37.81 and average weekly earnings were $1,300.66, at 34.4 hours. Retail trade averaged $26.38 an hour, or $807.23 a week. Leisure and hospitality averaged $23.90 an hour, or $609.45 a week. The May 2025 median for customer service representatives was $21.53 an hour, or $44,770 a year. The lowest 10% of that occupation earned under $15.27, and the highest 10% over $30.57. Entry-level remote call-center ads have been clustering near $15 to $17, which is the bottom of that range, not the private-economy average.

Where a posting has to show a range

A compilation of official pages, current as of 13 October 2026, found pay-range rules in Colorado (1 January 2021), New York City (1 November 2022), California and Washington (1 January 2023), New York State (17 September 2023), Hawaii (1 January 2024), the District of Columbia (30 June 2024), Maryland (1 October 2024), Illinois and Minnesota (1 January 2025), New Jersey (1 June 2025), Vermont (1 July 2025), Massachusetts (29 October 2025), Virginia (1 July 2026), Maine (29 July 2026), and Connecticut (1 October 2026). Thresholds differ by employer size and by whether the work is done in the state. In that compiler’s own postings on 1 November 2026, 69.5% stated a range inside those jurisdictions and 32.6% stated one elsewhere. This list is a map of public rules, not legal advice.

Employee or contractor, still unsettled on paper

Some online offers are written as independent-contractor work. The comparison with a job includes overtime, unemployment insurance, and who pays the employer share of Social Security. On 26 February 2026 the Department of Labor proposed to replace its 2024 classification guidance with a modified economic-reality test. Comments closed on 28 April 2026. On 28 October 2026 that final rule had cleared White House review, and as of this review date it had no publication date. Until publication, the 2024 guidance is what the Department has published. Courts apply their own case law. State tests, including California’s ABC test, are separate. This is a status note, not a classification of any engagement.
