About

What Weekline is, and what it isn’t.

Weekline is a set of guides on U.S. careers, pay, training, flexible work, and extra income. It is general information you can read before you compare a job, a program, or a side gig — not a place to apply.

What you’ll find here

The career guides cover where jobs are projected to grow, how to look up pay near you, and how to spot a fake listing. Work options compares remote, hybrid, part-time, freelance, and gig arrangements, and what to ask before you accept one. Extra income looks at how a second job or side gig can differ in hours, costs, and how steadily the work comes in.

When a page uses a figure, it names the source — usually the Bureau of Labor Statistics, CareerOneStop, Apprenticeship.gov, the Federal Reserve, or the Federal Trade Commission. Figures can change, and pay and requirements vary by employer and location, so check the date and confirm details with the original source or the employer.

Who it’s for

These guides are for people comparing a career move, a training path, or a more flexible schedule, including a first job, a change of field, or extra income on the side. You do not need a specialist vocabulary to read them.

What Weekline does not do

Weekline is an independent publisher. It is not an employer, a staffing agency, or a job board. It does not list openings, take applications, screen employers, or place candidates. Reading a page does not sign you up for anything.

The content is not legal, tax, financial, or employment advice, and it does not tell you which job or program to choose. A question about a specific role belongs with that employer. For a topic you’d like us to cover next, use the contact form.
