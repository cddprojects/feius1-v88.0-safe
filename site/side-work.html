Second income

Two jobs, and the wider side-work surveys

A second paycheck is common in conversation and less common in the official job count. The gap is the definition.

Weekline · Record · Reviewed 3 November 2026

In short

  • The official multiple-job rate was 5.5% in September 2026.
  • A side-hustle survey that year found 27% of adults, on a wider definition, with a median of $200 a month.
  • A still wider question, any side income in the past year, produced 47% in a different survey.
  • The three figures do not combine into one rate.

The official count

In September 2026, not seasonally adjusted, 9.00 million people held more than one job. That was 5.5% of employed people. Men were at 4.7% and women at 6.4%. Women ages 20 to 24 were at 9.0%. The usual pattern was a full-time main job plus a part-time second job: 5.199 million people. Both jobs part time accounted for 2.250 million. Two full-time jobs were 421,000. Hours that vary accounted for 1.066 million.

Side work, defined more loosely

Bankrate’s YouGov survey of 2–4 June 2025, with 2,616 adults and 699 people who had a side hustle, defined the activity as extra money beyond the main source of income. Twenty-seven percent said they had one, down from 36% in 2024 and 39% in 2023. Gen Z ages 18 to 28 were at 34%, millennials 29 to 44 at 31%, Gen X 45 to 60 at 23%, and boomers 61 to 79 at 22%. The average was $885 a month and the median was $200. Fifty-eight percent earned more than $100, 25% more than $500, 14% more than $1,000, and 28% earned $1 to $50. Uses, for at least some of the money, were discretionary spending 41%, living expenses 35%, saving 28%, and debt 20%. Twenty-nine percent thought they would always need one. Sixteen percent wanted it to become the main income, and 21% of Gen Z side hustlers said that.

Types, among people who had one: none of 12 listed types 43%, online sales 15%, professional services 14%, food delivery 9%, crafts 9%, pet care 7%, teaching 6%, landscaping 4%, childcare 4%, healthcare 4%, fitness 3%, rideshare 3%, and home rental 2%.

Intuit QuickBooks surveyed 3,000 adults in December 2025. Forty-seven percent said they had earned any side income in the past 12 months, 56% of Gen Z and 54% of millennials. Among earners, online selling was 30% and freelancing 22%. The write-up’s monthly and hourly figures are a self-report ratio, not a wage, and they are not comparable to the Bureau of Labor Statistics average of $37.81. Forty percent said they planned to start a business or a side hustle in 2026. That survey and the Bankrate survey asked different questions. Neither replaces the 5.5% count of people who hold two jobs.

Bureau of Labor Statistics, September 2026, multiple jobholders, not seasonally adjusted. Bankrate press release on the 2–4 June 2025 YouGov survey. Intuit QuickBooks, December 2025. A vendor survey of hourly workers in North America is a different population and is not used here as a national rate.
