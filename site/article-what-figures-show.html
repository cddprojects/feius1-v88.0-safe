The figures

What the public figures show

Job Compass · General information · Last reviewed 2 November 2026

Three careful sources describe late 2026. They do not measure the same thing.

The short version

  • One series counts paid days at home.
  • One series counts people who teleworked in a single week.
  • One series counts ads, not workers.
  • A preference among office workers is a fourth, smaller group.

Paid days at home

The Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes asks about the share of full paid working days done at home. The sample is workers who earned at least $10,000 in a prior year, reweighted toward the Current Population Survey. The share was 7.2% in December 2019, about 62% in May 2020, and 26.8% in September 2026. It has stayed in the mid-20s since 2023.

In August 2026 the same project reported, for full-time wage-and-salary workers, 60.8% fully on site, 27.1% hybrid, and 12.1% fully remote. Home days were more common in information, finance, and professional services than in manufacturing, education, or transportation.

Anyone who teleworked that week

The Bureau of Labor Statistics asks people who worked during the survey week whether they teleworked or worked at home for pay. In September 2026, not seasonally adjusted, 21.9% had done so for at least some hours and 10.7% had done so for all of their hours. That is 34.7 million people out of 158.5 million at work. Women were at 23.9% and men at 20.1%. People with their own children under 18 were at 25.9%. People with a disability were at 24.4%.

Age runs against the idea that the youngest workers are the ones at home. Ages 16 to 24 were at 5.7%, because many of those jobs are on site. Ages 35 to 44 were at 26.2%.

Ads, unemployment, and second jobs

In September 2026 the seasonally adjusted unemployment rate was 4.2%, with 7.1 million people unemployed. Labor force participation was 61.8%. Part time for economic reasons was 4.5 million people. Multiple jobholders were 9.0 million, or 5.5% of the employed, in the not-seasonally-adjusted count. Most of them had a full-time main job and a part-time second job.

FRED series WFHCOVIDMATQUESTION and the SWAA release. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Employment Situation and table A-41, September 2026. Cite Barrero, Bloom, and Davis, NBER Working Paper 28731, when using the home-day series.
