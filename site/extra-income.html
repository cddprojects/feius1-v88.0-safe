Extra Income

What could a second job or side gig add to your budget?

A weekend shift, a freelance project, and app-based gig work can each bring in extra income. They also make very different demands on your time.

A part-time shift usually comes with set hours and a predictable hourly rate. A freelance project can pay more per hour of actual work, but also involves unpaid time finding clients, scoping work, and sending invoices. A delivery or rideshare gig offers flexible timing, but involves driving and vehicle expenses that reduce what you actually keep.

In September 2026, 9.00 million people (5.5% of employed people) held more than one job, most often a full-time main job plus a part-time second one. Separately, in a 2024 Federal Reserve survey of household finances, some gig workers said they would struggle to make ends meet without that income, and many said they wanted more predictable pay — a reminder that "extra income" ranges from a planned supplement to a necessary part of covering expenses.

Before committing time to any option, it's worth comparing: how often you'll actually get paid, what the work costs you in gas, mileage, supplies, or your own tools, and how consistent the hours or assignments tend to be. If you need a predictable amount each month, ask directly about consistency before you commit rather than assuming it.

This is general information about how these options typically work, not tax or financial advice. What you actually keep after taxes and expenses depends on your own situation — a tax professional can give figures specific to you.

Sources: Bureau of Labor Statistics, multiple jobholders, September 2026 (not seasonally adjusted). Federal Reserve, Report on the Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households, 2024.
