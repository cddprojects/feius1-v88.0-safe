Pay

Pay, minimum wages, and employee or contractor status

Job Compass · General information · Last reviewed 2 November 2026

An hourly figure means little until the reader knows the state, the hours, and who is responsible for taxes.

The short version

  • The federal minimum wage is still $7.25 an hour.
  • Many states require more, and the higher rate is the one that applies.
  • National average earnings sit far above entry-level remote ads.
  • Calling work a contract does not, by itself, decide the legal status.

The wage floor

The federal minimum has been $7.25 an hour since 24 July 2009. Employers covered by the Fair Labor Standards Act pay the highest of the federal, state, and local rates. The Department of Labor’s consolidated table dated 1 July 2026 lists 30 states plus the District of Columbia above $7.25. Examples from that table include Washington at $17.13, California at $16.90, New York City and Long Island at $17.00, and upstate New York at $16.00. Texas and Pennsylvania are in the federal column. A note on the same page still describes the District of Columbia as $17.50, while the table row says $18.40, so the District figure should be confirmed with the District before anyone relies on it.

What people already earn

In September 2026, preliminary and seasonally adjusted, average hourly earnings for all private employees were $37.81, and average weekly earnings were $1,300.66. Retail trade averaged $26.38 an hour. Leisure and hospitality averaged $23.90. These are averages across the whole private economy. They are not the wage on a particular posting. The May 2025 median for customer service representatives was $21.53 an hour, or $44,770 a year. The lowest 10% of that occupation earned under $15.27. Entry-level remote call-center ads often land near that lower end.

Employee or contractor

Some online and flexible offers are written as independent-contractor work. Readers comparing them with a job are comparing overtime, unemployment insurance, and who pays the employer share of Social Security, not only the hourly rate. On 26 February 2026 the Department of Labor proposed to replace its 2024 classification guidance with a modified economic-reality test. On 28 October 2026 that final rule had cleared White House review and, as of this review date, had no publication date. State tests are separate. This paragraph is a status note for readers. It is not advice about any one engagement.

U.S. Department of Labor, minimum wage and the consolidated table effective 1 July 2026. Bureau of Labor Statistics, table B-3, September 2026, and the Occupational Outlook Handbook for customer service representatives, May 2025. Federal Register, 27 February 2026. OIRA record RIN 1235-AA46, concluded 28 October 2026.
