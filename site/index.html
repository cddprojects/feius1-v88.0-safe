Remote, online, and flexible work.

Three kinds of work people talk about as if they were one. Each word describes a different part of the week.

Where

Remote

The work happens away from the usual workplace. Often that means home. Some people are home all week. Others are home on some days and at the workplace on others. A split week is often called hybrid.

Home is a place. A person can work from home and still keep a fixed start and finish time.

How

Online

The work travels over the internet. It might be a video lesson, a customer chat, a call on a headset, or a document sent when it is finished.

Online tells you how the work moves. It does not tell you whether the desk is at home or in an office, or who chooses the hours.

When

Flexible

The person has some say over the clock. They might start later, keep the week short, or work in the evening around school, family, or another job.

The say can be small. If someone else assigns the shift, the hours are still fixed, even when the work is done at home.

Ordinary weeks

How the words show up together.

Remote and online

A daytime week at home

Customer questions are answered on a laptop, from home, on a set schedule such as 9 to 5. The week is remote because of the place, and online because of the laptop. The hours were assigned, so the week is not flexible.

Online

A week at the office

The person sits at the workplace, finishes a report, and sends it to the team. The work moved online. The person did not. This week is not remote.

Online, flexible, and remote

A few evenings of tutoring

Lessons happen on a video call, a few evenings a week, around school or another responsibility. The hours are chosen, the lesson is online, and the tutor is at home. All three words fit this week.

If you want the counts

Where full-time weeks happened in August 2026.

About 12 in 100 full-time employees worked only from home. About 27 in 100 split the week between home and a workplace. About 61 in 100 worked only at the workplace. The count is of weeks, not a promise about any one person.
