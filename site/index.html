Three ordinary questions

How people search, how they apply, and what the ad actually requires.

Weekline is a reader’s companion, not a job board. These pages answer three plain questions about remote, online, and flexible work in the United States: where the looking happens, how an application usually runs, and what you’re actually asked to have before you’re hired.

“Apply online” hides a few steps worth knowing in advance: a form that usually takes 10 to 15 minutes, a handful of screening questions that can end the application right there, sometimes a short skills test, and then, if it goes well, a request for the same few things almost every U.S. employer needs: proof you can legally work, a working computer and internet connection, and the tax paperwork that comes with being an employee or a contractor. None of that is unusual. Knowing it in advance just means fewer surprises.
