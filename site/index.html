The search

Where, how, and when Americans search for remote, online, and flexible work.

Weekline is a reader’s companion, not a job board. Before the pay and the fine print, here is where people actually look, how a search tends to run, and when the remote ads are thin or plentiful.

“Just search online” skips the parts that decide whether a search goes anywhere. Which site a person opens first, what they check before they apply, and how many of the ads they find are genuinely remote all change by the month and by the field. This note walks through where the looking starts, the five or so steps a typical search runs through, and when remote postings are scarce versus when they loosen up, using the public surveys and hiring-site data behind each figure.

Where

The search starts on the big boards, not a single site.

General job boards, LinkedIn, niche boards, and the employer’s own site are all in play at once. Which one comes first depends on whether the search is for a professional role or an hourly one.

How

A search tends to run the same five or so steps.

Search a title plus “remote” or a city, screen out the ads missing pay or an employer name, check the company site, apply where the form is short, and keep a second, non-ad path for extra cash.

When

Remote ads are a thin, shifting slice of the board.

The remote-or-hybrid share of U.S. postings peaked near 10% in 2022 and has sat near 8% since 2024, lower in some fields and far higher in others. Applications to remote ads outrun the ads themselves.
