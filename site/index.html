Before you say yes

Four signs the offer’s not real.

Folks lost over $500 million to job scams in 2024. These are the patterns regulators keep catching — not a reason to assume every offer’s a scam, just stuff worth knowing.

🚩

No company name

Vague or unnamed company? That’s the single biggest red flag seekers and fraud reports keep pointing to.

🚩

They want money upfront

A “kit fee,” a training fee, a deposit before you’ve even started — doesn’t matter how it’s dressed up, it’s never legit.

🚩

They send you a check

Deposit this check, then wire part of it back — that’s the single most common scam move the FTC tracks.

🚩

You need to decide right now

A real offer can survive you sleeping on it. If they’re rushing you, that’s a tactic, not how real hiring works.

Quick answers

Before you open another tab.

These are the questions almost everyone has but almost nobody asks out loud. None of them are too obvious — promise. The full guides go deeper, with sources.

See every note →

Do I have to pay anything to apply?

Nope. A legit employer never charges you to apply, never sells you a “starter kit” or training, and never asks you to deposit a check and wire part back. Any of that means it’s a scam, full stop.

Do I need special equipment to work from home?

Usually, yeah — a computer that meets some basic specs and a solid internet connection, sometimes a wired connection instead of a hotspot. A real employer ships you the gear or pays you back for it. They don’t make you buy it yourself first.

Am I an employee or a contractor?

Look at which tax form you’re handed. A W-4 means you’re a W-2 employee and taxes come out of every paycheck. A W-9 means you’re a 1099 contractor handling your own taxes.

How long should an application actually take?

About half of job seekers say 10 to 15 minutes feels fair for an online form. If yours is dragging way longer, or making you retype a résumé you already uploaded, that’s a known annoyance — not you doing something wrong.
