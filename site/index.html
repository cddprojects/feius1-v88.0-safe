General information for readers

Remote, online, and flexible work in the United States.

A plain account of what those words describe, where people look, and what public figures say about pay, hours, and home-based work.

  • Three separate ideas. Remote is a place. Online is a way of doing the work. Flexible is a schedule.
  • Public figures. Each survey covers a different group, so one percentage does not describe every worker.
  • A readable posting. Readers can look for the employer, the place, the hours, and the pay.
  • This site. Job Compass explains the terms. It does not list jobs or take applications.

Three work arrangements

WhereRemoteAway from the usual workplace. HowOnlineDone or delivered over the internet. WhenFlexibleHours chosen around other commitments.
Work arrangements

The words on a posting are not interchangeable.

A job can be online and still require a set office. It can be flexible and still happen on a shop floor.

Remote and hybridWork done away from the employer’s usual site, or split between home and that site. Online workWork completed or handed in over the internet, from home or from a workplace. Flexible hoursSome choice over the schedule. The choice may be small, and the shift may still be assigned. A second streamAnother job or occasional paid work beside a main job, study, or caregiving.
What the figures describe

A few public measures, kept separate.

These numbers answer different questions. They are a starting point for reading, not a prediction for any one person.

  1. 26.8%

    Paid days at home

    Share of full paid working days done at home in September 2026, in the Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes.

  2. 21.9%

    Any telework that week

    Share of people at work in the September 2026 reference week who teleworked at least some hours, from the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

  3. 8.3%

    Ads that mention it

    Share of U.S. Indeed postings that mentioned remote or hybrid work on 31 January 2025, down from 10.4% in February 2022.

Sources are named in What the public figures show. Surveys use different questions and different groups of people.
