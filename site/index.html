General information, not job listings

72% of job seekers ran into a scam this year. Here’s how to tell before you don’t.

Almost three in four U.S. job seekers hit a fraudulent posting, a fake recruiter, or a request for payment during their search last year, and only 1% say they trust online listings completely. This explains what a real hiring process actually looks like — where people search, how an application really moves, and what employers legitimately ask for — so it’s easier to spot what doesn’t fit.

Suspicion isn’t paranoia here — the data backs it up. Most people are given no explanation of how real hiring works, which is exactly what makes a fake offer hard to catch.

Before you say yes

Four signs an offer isn’t real.

Reported job-scam losses topped $500 million in 2024, and most job seekers now encounter at least one scam per search. These four patterns show up most often in that reporting — not a reason to doubt every offer, just what regulators see repeated most.

🚩

No employer name

If the company won’t say who they are, that’s the single biggest red flag job seekers and fraud reports keep pointing to.

🚩

They want money upfront

A kit fee, a training fee, a “deposit” before you’ve even started — however it’s framed, it’s never legitimate.

🚩

They send you a check to deposit

Deposit this check, then send part of it back — that’s the single most common scam the FTC tracks.

🚩

They want an answer right now

A real offer can survive you sleeping on it. If they’re rushing you, that’s the tactic, not how real hiring works.

Quick answers

Probably already on your mind.

You’re likely wondering about at least one of these. None of them are too basic to ask — here’s the short version, and the full guides go deeper with sources.

See every note →

Do I need to pay anything to apply?

No. A real employer never charges you to apply, never sells you a “starter kit” or training, and never asks you to deposit a check and send part of it back. If any of that happens, you’re looking at a scam.

Will I need specific equipment to work from home?

Usually, yes — a computer that meets some basic specs and a reliable internet connection, sometimes a wired one instead of a hotspot. A real employer ships you the equipment or pays you back for it. They don’t make you buy it yourself first.

Am I an employee or a contractor?

The tax form used is the reliable signal: a W-4 means a W-2 employee with taxes withheld from every paycheck, while a W-9 means a 1099 contractor handling their own taxes.

How long should an application actually take?

About half of job seekers say 10 to 15 minutes feels fair for an online form. If yours is taking a lot longer, or making you retype a résumé you already uploaded, that’s a known annoyance — not something you’re doing wrong.
