Remote, online, and flexible work.

Three different parts of the same week.

Where

Remote

Work done away from the usual workplace, or split between home and that workplace.

12.1% fully remoteAmong full-time employees in August 2026, 27.1% were hybrid and 60.8% were fully on site.

How

Online

Work done or handed in over the internet. The person may be at home or in an office.

Delivered over the internetA video call, a chat, or a file can be sent from home or from an office. The word leaves the hours open.

When

Flexible

Some choice over the schedule. The choice can be small. A shift can still be assigned.

62% had little controlIn early 2025, 62% of workers lacked a predictable, stable schedule they controlled.

One week

A week can include one word, two, or all three.

From home, on a set shift

Remote and online

The person is away from the workplace and uses the internet. The hours are still assigned.

In the office, sent over the internet

Online

The work moves online. The workplace is still the office.

Chosen hours, still at a workplace

Flexible

The person has some say over the clock. The work can still happen on site.

August 2026 shares are for full-time wage-and-salary workers, from the Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes. The 62% figure is from the American Job Quality Study, January to February 2025. A longer note on the three words.

What this page is for

Weekline explains remote, online, and flexible work in plain language. It is general information for readers in the United States.

Weekline does not list jobs or take applications. It does not say which arrangement fits a particular household, and it does not give legal, tax, or employment advice.
