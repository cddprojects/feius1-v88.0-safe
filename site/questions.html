Questions

What this record is

Answers about the publication. They are not answers about a particular job.

Does Weekline list jobs or take applications?

No. The pages record public figures, definitions, and the shape of the ad market. Hiring is between a person and an employer or a client.

Is this a guide to choosing an arrangement?

No. The pages do not tell a reader which arrangement suits their hours, their household, or their training. They separate facts that are easy to treat as one fact.

Is this legal, tax, or employment advice?

No. Wage rules, tax withholding, and worker classification depend on the facts and on federal, state, and local law. Each note names the office that publishes the figure.

Why do the percentages disagree?

They measure different things. A share of paid days, a share of people who teleworked in one week, a share of job ads, and a share of adults who report a side hustle are not one statistic. Days, people, and ads keeps them apart.

Does remote work mean the worker chooses the hours?

No. Many home-based customer-service roles assign a shift. Hours are a separate fact. Place, delivery, and hours.

How current are the figures?

Each note has a review date and names its sources. Labor figures move. The agency page is the place to confirm a number.
