Glossary

Terms readers meet on postings

Short definitions. Legal duties depend on the statute, the state, and the facts of the job.

No matching term.
Remote
Work done away from the employer’s usual workplace. The posting may still limit the states or time zones.
Hybrid
Some days remote and some days at a set site. Regular office days mean the job is not fully remote.
Online work
Work done or delivered over the internet. The word does not settle the place, the hours, or the employment status.
Flexible schedule
Some choice over hours. The choice can be small. An assigned shift worked from home is still an assigned shift.
Part-time
In Bureau of Labor Statistics household data, fewer than 35 hours a week. It is a count of hours, not a type of pay.
Multiple jobholder
A person counted by the Bureau of Labor Statistics as holding more than one job. In September 2026 that was 5.5% of employed people.
Side hustle
A survey term for extra money beside a main source of income. It is wider than the official count of second jobs. Definitions differ by survey.
W-2 employee
A worker whose employer withholds income tax and pays the employer share of Social Security and Medicare. The label on a posting is not the legal test.
1099 contractor
A worker paid without employee withholding, often described as an independent contractor. Status depends on the economic reality of the work and on state rules.
Minimum wage
The lowest hourly rate the law requires. The federal rate is $7.25. A higher state or city rate controls when it applies.
Median wage
The point where half of workers in that occupation earn more and half earn less. It is not the wage on one posting, and it is not the average.
Telework
In the Current Population Survey, any work at home for pay during the reference week. It includes people who did this for only some of their hours.
Pay range
The wage or salary stated on a posting. Some states require a range for covered employers and jobs. Readers also look for it where no statute requires it.
Task scam
A fraud pattern described by the Federal Trade Commission: simple online tasks, a small early payout, then a demand to deposit money to withdraw supposed earnings.
