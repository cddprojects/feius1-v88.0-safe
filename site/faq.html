FAQ

Questions readers ask about these guides

Answers about the site. They are not answers about a particular job.

Does Job Compass list jobs or take applications?

No. The pages explain terms, public figures, and what a readable posting contains. Hiring is between a reader and an employer or client.

Is this legal, tax, or employment advice?

No. Wage rules, tax withholding, and worker classification depend on the facts and on federal, state, and local law. The guides point to the public office that publishes each figure.

Why do the percentages disagree?

They measure different things. A share of paid days, a share of people who teleworked in one week, a share of job ads, and a share of adults who say they have a side hustle are not one statistic. What the public figures show keeps them apart.

Does remote work mean the worker chooses the hours?

No. Many home-based customer-service roles assign a shift. Flexible hours are a separate condition. How the words differ.

What should a reader do with a posting that hides the employer?

Treat the missing name as a reason to stop. The Federal Trade Commission describes unnamed remote offers, upfront fees, and fake checks as common job-fraud patterns. How to read a posting.

How current are the figures?

Each guide has a review date and names its sources. Labor figures move. The linked agency page is the place to confirm a number before relying on it.
