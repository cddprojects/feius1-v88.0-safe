The record

Days, people, and ads are three counts

Late 2026 has three careful sources. They do not measure the same object.

Weekline · Record · Reviewed 3 November 2026

In short

  • One series counts paid days at home.
  • One series counts people who teleworked in a single week.
  • One series counts ads, not workers.
  • A preference among people whose jobs can be remote is a fourth, smaller group.

Paid days at home

The Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes asks about the share of full paid working days done at home. The sample is workers who earned at least $10,000 in a prior year, reweighted toward the Current Population Survey. The share was 7.2% in December 2019, about 62% in May 2020, and 26.8% in September 2026. It has stayed in the mid-20s since 2023.

In August 2026 the same project reported, for full-time wage-and-salary workers, 60.8% fully on site, 27.1% hybrid, and 12.1% fully remote. Home days were more common in information, finance, and professional services than in manufacturing, education, or transportation. Women were at about 29.3% of paid days at home that month, and men at about 25.2%.

Anyone who teleworked that week

The Bureau of Labor Statistics asks people who worked during the survey week whether they teleworked or worked at home for pay. In September 2026, not seasonally adjusted, 21.9% had done so for at least some hours and 10.7% had done so for all of their hours. That is 34.7 million people out of 158.5 million at work. Women were at 23.9% and men at 20.1%. People with their own children under 18 were at 25.9%. People with a disability were at 24.4%.

Age runs against the idea that the youngest workers are the ones at home. Ages 16 to 24 were at 5.7%, because many of those jobs are on site. Ages 35 to 44 were at 26.2%. Among people 25 and older, the any-telework share was 4.4% with no high school diploma, 8.3% with a high school diploma only, 18.1% with some college, 37.8% with a bachelor’s degree, and 40.6% with an advanced degree.

The same month’s household survey, seasonally adjusted, put unemployment at 4.2% (7.1 million people). Labor-force participation was 61.8%. Employment as a share of the population was 59.2%. Payrolls rose by 29,000. Part time for economic reasons was 4.501 million. People unemployed 27 weeks or longer numbered 1.944 million.

Preference is a smaller room

Gallup’s location figures cover employees whose work can be done remotely. In August 2026 that group was 54% hybrid, 25% exclusively remote, and 21% on site. In November 2025, preference in the same kind of group was 61% hybrid, 33% remote, and 6% on site. In May 2025, Gen Z was the least likely to want fully remote work: 23%, against 35% of older employees, with 71% preferring hybrid.

Ads are a third object

Indeed’s count of postings that mention remote or hybrid work was 2.6% in January 2019, 10.4% at the February 2022 peak, 7.8% on 31 October 2024, and 8.3% on 31 January 2025. A secondary compilation of that tracker put the share at 8.4% on 31 August 2026. LinkedIn told Business Insider that in September 2025 about 8% of paid U.S. postings were remote and those postings drew 35% of applications. July 2025 was about 9% of postings and 37% of applications. The ad share and the application share are both real, and they are not a count of people who work from home.
