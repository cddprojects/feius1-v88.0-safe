How remote, online, and flexible work differ

Job Compass · General information · Last reviewed 2 November 2026

The three words often sit in the same sentence. They still answer different questions.

The short version

  • Remote describes where the work is done.
  • Online describes how the work is delivered.
  • Flexible describes the hours or the workload.
  • A job can include one of them, or all three.

Where

Remote work happens away from the employer’s usual site. Hybrid mixes home days and site days. Among full-time wage-and-salary workers in August 2026, the Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes put 12.1% fully remote, 27.1% hybrid, and 60.8% fully on site. Gallup’s figures are higher because they cover employees whose jobs could be done remotely, not the whole workforce. In August 2026 that group was 25% fully remote, 54% hybrid, and 21% on site.

How

Online work is done or turned in over the internet. A hospital coder, a tutor on a video call, and a person answering calls from a spare room are all doing online work. The coder may have a certificate and a set caseload. The tutor may choose evening hours. The call-center shift may be assigned between set clock times. The word “online” does not choose among those.

When

Flexible work offers some say over the schedule. Part-time work is a count of hours, not a promise of choice. The Bureau of Labor Statistics treats fewer than 35 hours as part time. A part-time data-entry shift that happens every Monday is regular. It is not the same thing as a contract whose hours follow a client.

Why the split matters for a reader

Someone reducing a commute is asking about place. Someone fitting work around school or caregiving is asking about hours. Someone comparing a paycheck with a contract is asking about status and tax. A posting that uses all three words and then skips the place, the hours, and the status has not answered the question.

Survey of Working Arrangements and Attitudes via FRED, August 2026 arrangement shares. Gallup Hybrid Work indicator, August 2026. Bureau of Labor Statistics definition of full time: 35 hours or more.
