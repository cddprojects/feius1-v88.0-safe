About

Weekline makes this easy to understand.

Remote, online, and independent work come with a process almost nobody explains plainly. Weekline explains it, in everyday language, before a reader says yes to any of it.

What you’ll learn here

Remote is the place. Online is how the work travels. Flexible is the schedule. Independent is who you answer to. Each page keeps those apart using everyday situations, then points to the public figure behind a number when one is used.

You don’t need to already know the vocabulary or have done any of this before. If a term, a form, or a step anywhere on this site isn’t explained clearly, with an example, that’s on us to fix — not something you missed.

Weekline is an independent publisher. It is not an employer, a staffing agency, or a job board. It does not list openings and it does not take applications. Reading a page does not sign you up for anything.

Who this is for

Anyone trying remote, online, or independent work for the first time, or anyone who wants a plain answer before taking an offer that sounds too easy. No prior experience with any of it is assumed.

The limit

These pages don’t make the decision for you, and they don’t replace the Department of Labor, the Internal Revenue Service, a state labor office, or the Federal Trade Commission.

Weekline publishes general information for reading. It has no application form. A question about a particular job belongs with the employer who offers it.
