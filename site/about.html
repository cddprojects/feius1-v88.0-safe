About

A record, not a search.

Paid Day sets published U.S. figures next to the ad market. It stops at the record.

What is being recorded

The subject is the distance between three things: how Americans spend paid days, how many people telework in a given week, and how job ads use the words remote, online, and flexible. State wage floors and pay-range rules are part of that record because they change the meaning of a number on a posting.

Paid Day is an independent publisher. It is not a government agency, not an employer, and not an employment agency. It does not list jobs, receive applications, or send reader information to employers.

The unit

A paid day, a person in a survey week, and an advertisement are different units. The name of the publication is the first of those units, because that series is the one most often mixed with the others.

The limit

The record does not rank arrangements for a reader’s life, and it does not replace the Department of Labor, the Internal Revenue Service, a state labor office, or the Federal Trade Commission.

Paid Day publishes general information. It has no application inbox. A question about a specific posting belongs with the employer named on that posting, or with the agency that wrote the rule.
