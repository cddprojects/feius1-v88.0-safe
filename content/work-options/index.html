Work Options

Remote, hybrid, or flexible hours?

A job can offer flexibility in where you work, when you work, or both. Here is what each common arrangement usually means, and what to ask before you accept one.

In 2025, 35% of employed people did some or all of their work at home on days they worked, while 70% did some or all of their work at their workplace; these overlap because many people split time between the two. "Flexible" does not describe one single arrangement — it can mean where you work, when you work, or both, and the specifics are worth confirming before you accept an offer.

Work optionWhat to check
Part-time jobsExpected weekly hours, shift changes, and which benefits are available.
Remote jobsEligible states, time zone requirements, and whether travel is expected.
Hybrid jobsRequired office days, commute time, and who sets the schedule.
Flexible schedulesRequired hours, how far ahead shifts are posted, and whether you can change your availability.
Freelance workProject scope, payment terms, and the time it takes to find clients.
App-based gigsPay per task, unpaid waiting time, and expenses such as mileage or supplies.

Source: BLS American Time Use Survey, 2025 annual averages.

Practical guides: what to ask before you apply or accept an offer

How to ask for a flexible schedule

Put together a short, written proposal: the schedule you’re asking for, how you’ll cover your responsibilities during that schedule, and a date when you and your manager will check in on how it’s working. A concrete plan is easier to say yes to than an open-ended request.

Employee or independent contractor?

Before accepting, ask how you’ll be paid, who covers equipment and work-related expenses, and which benefits, if any, come with the arrangement. Which tax form you’re asked to complete is a reliable signal: a W-4 points to employee status with taxes withheld from each paycheck, while a W-9 points to independent-contractor status, where you’re responsible for your own tax payments.

How to write a résumé for a remote role

Show, with specific examples, how you’ve handled deadlines independently, communicated progress without in-person check-ins, and worked with people in different locations or time zones. Concrete outcomes read as more convincing than general claims of being "self-motivated" or "a strong communicator."
